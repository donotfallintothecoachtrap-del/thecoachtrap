Getting Clients

Q1

How do I get my first paying coaching client?

Start with the people who already trust you — your existing network. Reach out personally to 10–15 people who know your work and tell them exactly who you're helping and what outcome you deliver. Don't pitch; have a conversation. Your first client almost always comes from a warm connection, not a cold funnel.

Q2

How do I get coaching clients without paid advertising?

Organic client attraction comes from three sources: content that shows you understand your client's problem, community presence where you answer questions and build trust, and direct conversation with people who are a good fit. Most coaches skip the third one. Personal outreach — not mass messaging, but genuine one-to-one conversation — is still the fastest route to a client without spending on ads.

Q3

How do I attract high-ticket coaching clients?

High-ticket clients aren't found in different places — they're attracted by different positioning. They buy certainty, not possibility. Your messaging needs to show a clear, specific outcome with evidence it works. Stop talking about the coaching process and start talking about the life or business on the other side of it.

Q4

How do I convert followers into coaching clients?

Followers become clients when they trust you enough to take the next step. That trust is built through consistent, specific content — and the conversion happens through conversation. Your content creates the context; a direct message, a discovery call, or a clear CTA creates the client. Most coaches rely entirely on content and never make the ask. Make the ask.

Q5

How do I stop relying on word of mouth and build a predictable pipeline?

Word of mouth is great but unpredictable. Predictable pipeline comes from having a defined, repeatable way to attract, nurture, and convert ideal clients — a specific piece of content that introduces you, a community or list you own, and a clear next step that moves people from interested to enrolled. Build that system once and refine it over time, rather than starting from scratch each month.

Pricing & Income

Q6

How do I price my coaching without undercharging?

Price based on the outcome you deliver, not the hours you spend. If your coaching helps a client build a sustainable business, that's worth far more than £100 an hour. Start by asking: what is the financial or life value of the result I help clients achieve? Then price at a fraction of that value — and hold the number.

Q7

How do I stop undercharging for my coaching?

Undercharging is usually a confidence problem disguised as a pricing problem. The fix is two-part: get clear on the genuine value of your outcome (not your time), and collect evidence that your coaching works. Case studies, testimonials, and client results are what give you the internal permission to charge what you're worth.

Q8

Why do coaches struggle to make consistent income?

Most coaches build their business around activity — posting, networking, being busy — rather than around a clear offer and a repeatable way to attract clients. Inconsistent income is almost always the symptom of an unclear message and no defined client pathway. Fix the foundation, and the income follows.

Q9

How long does it take to build a profitable coaching business?

With a clear niche, a specific offer, and consistent outreach, most coaches can sign their first clients within 30–60 days. A sustainably profitable business — one that generates reliable monthly income without constant hustle — typically takes 6–18 months to build. The timeline almost always comes down to the clarity of your message, not the quality of your coaching.

Niche & Positioning

Q10

How do I stand out in a crowded coaching market?

Specificity beats visibility every time. The coaches who stand out aren't the loudest — they're the clearest. When you can describe your ideal client's exact problem better than they can describe it themselves, you become the obvious choice. Niche down until it feels uncomfortably specific, then niche down a little more.

Q11

How do I find my niche as a coach?

Your niche is at the intersection of three things: a problem you understand deeply, a client you genuinely want to help, and a result you can reliably deliver. Most coaches try to find their niche by thinking about it. You'll find it faster by working with real clients, paying attention to who you do your best work with, and doubling down on that. Your niche finds you through action, not reflection.

Q12

How do I know if my coaching niche is specific enough?

If you can describe your ideal client's situation so precisely that they think you've been reading their diary, your niche is specific enough. If your description could apply to 10,000 different people, you need to go narrower. Test it: write a post about your client's exact problem and see if the right people feel seen by it.

Q13

How do I create a coaching offer that sells?

A great coaching offer solves one specific problem for one specific person and delivers a clear, named outcome. It should be easy to explain in one sentence: "I help [specific person] achieve [specific outcome] in [specific timeframe]." If you need a paragraph to explain what you do, your offer isn't clear enough yet.

Q14

How do I position myself as an expert in coaching?

You don't become an expert by calling yourself one — you become one by demonstrating expertise consistently. Share your thinking, not just your advice. Show your process. Take a stance on things other coaches won't say. The coaches who are seen as experts publish their point of view so consistently that their audience can predict what they'd say before they say it.

Marketing & Content

Q15

How do I market myself as a coach without feeling salesy?

Marketing feels salesy when it's about you. When it's about your client — their problem, their aspiration, their specific situation — it stops feeling like a pitch and starts feeling like a conversation. Share what you know, show what's possible, and let the right people self-select. You're not convincing anyone; you're helping the right people find you.

Q16

What should I post on social media as a coach?

Post answers to the questions your ideal clients are already asking. Every piece of content should do one of three things: show you understand their problem, demonstrate that change is possible, or give them a small win they couldn't get without you. If your content could have been written by any coach, it needs to be more specific.

Q17

How do I get clients from LinkedIn as a coach?

LinkedIn rewards consistency and specificity. Post three times a week — always starting with a question your ideal client is already asking themselves. Answer it in 3–4 sentences. End with a clear next step. Engage genuinely with other people's content. Over 90 days, this compounds into visibility with exactly the people who need what you offer.

Q18

What is the biggest mistake coaches make with their marketing?

Talking about coaching instead of talking about the client's problem. Coaches post about their methods, their frameworks, their certifications — and their ideal clients scroll past because they don't see themselves in it. The shift is simple: lead with the problem, follow with the possibility, and let your expertise speak through the specificity of your understanding, not the length of your credentials.

Q19

How do I create content consistently without burning out?

Batch your content creation and separate it from publishing. One two-hour session per week to write everything, then schedule it out. Build a Q&A bank — a living document of every question your clients ask — and treat it as your content library. You will never run out of things to say if you're paying attention to what your clients are actually asking.

Q20

How do I build a coaching business without social media?

You can build a strong coaching business entirely through referrals, strategic partnerships, speaking, and community presence — no social media required. The key is building relationships intentionally: who already has your clients, and how do you get in front of them? Social media is one distribution channel, not the only one.

Personal Brand & Authority

Q21

How do I build a personal brand as a coach?

Your personal brand is not your logo or your colour palette — it's your point of view. What do you believe about coaching, about business, about your clients that most people in your space wouldn't say out loud? Start there. Consistent, clear, opinionated content about the problems you solve is how coaches build brands that attract clients rather than just followers.

Q22

How do I write a coaching bio that actually attracts clients?

Your bio should be written for your ideal client, not for you. Start with the problem you solve or the transformation you create. Then explain how you do it and why you're the person to trust with it. End with a clear call to action. If your bio reads like a CV, rewrite it as a conversation.

Q23

How do I build an audience as a coach starting from scratch?

Start by going where your ideal clients already are — LinkedIn groups, Facebook communities, Reddit threads, industry forums. Contribute genuinely. Answer questions. Be useful without expecting anything in return. Audience building from scratch is slow at first and then suddenly fast. The people who win are the ones who stay consistent when nothing is happening yet.

Q24

How do I get testimonials and case studies for my coaching?

Ask every client for a testimonial at the end of your engagement — but make it easy by asking a specific question: "What was your situation before we worked together, what changed, and what would you say to someone considering coaching with me?" The best testimonials come from prompting, not from hoping clients will volunteer them.

Business Systems

Q25

How do I build a coaching business that doesn't depend entirely on me?

Start by creating systems that don't require you to reinvent the wheel every time: a clear intake process, a defined client journey, content that works while you sleep, and a community that builds connection without your constant presence. The goal isn't to remove yourself — it's to build something that grows whether you had a great week or a difficult one.
